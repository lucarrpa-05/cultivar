# Liberty upsets patterns

Robert Nozick, writing against Rawls in *Anarchy, State, and Utopia* (1974), offers a deal. Pick any distribution of wealth you consider perfectly just. Strict equality, the difference principle, anything — call it D1, and stipulate it is ideal.

Now: a million people each drop an extra 25 cents in a box to watch Wilt Chamberlain play basketball. Nobody is tricked or coerced, and every holding was just to begin with. Chamberlain ends the season with an extra \$250,000, and the distribution is now D2, unequal and by your own standard unjust.

Which transaction was wrong? Nozick's conclusion: no **patterned** principle — one demanding the distribution always match a formula — survives without forbidding voluntary transfers or continuously undoing them. Justice is therefore historical: a holding is just if acquired and transferred justly, whatever shape results.

G. A. Cohen's reply is that the argument assumes its baseline. The fans spend money held under one particular property regime, and whether Chamberlain may charge at all is exactly what was in dispute.
