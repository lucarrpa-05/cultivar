# Remember falsifiability? Here is a field that stopped doing it

Popper's demand was that a theory forbid something, and that you go looking for the thing it forbids. Statistical significance testing was supposed to be exactly that, industrialised: fix a prediction, run the experiment, let a small p-value count as a failed attempt to refute.

Then note what a career rewards. Choose the outcome measure after seeing the data. Drop the conditions that failed. Add participants until the test crosses the line. Write the introduction so the result you found is the one you predicted — Norbert Kerr named that HARKing in 1998. Each keeps the vocabulary of refutation while removing its possibility: the hypothesis is fitted to the data, not exposed to it.

The measurement arrived in 2015. The Open Science Collaboration ran 100 replications of psychology studies: 97% of the originals reported significant effects, and 36% of the replications did. John Ioannidis had predicted the shape of this in 2005 from the arithmetic of low power, small effects and many analytic choices.

The cure is old-fashioned. Preregistration is falsifiability as paperwork: say in advance what would count as failure, so the world can still say no.
