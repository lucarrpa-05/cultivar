# What would have to happen for you to give this up?

Vienna, 1919. Karl Popper, then a young man, was surrounded by theories that explained everything: Marxist history, Adler's psychology. A man pushes a child into a river; a man dies saving one. The theory accounts for both, easily, every time. That explanatory power impressed everyone else and worried him.

Then Eddington's eclipse expedition tested Einstein. Light bends by the predicted amount, or general relativity is finished. Einstein had staked it on something that could go wrong.

That is the criterion. A scientific theory forbids things, and the more it forbids the more it says. Confirmations are cheap: look and you will find them.

The criterion does not survive practice, and Popper knew it. You never test a hypothesis alone: it comes bundled with instruments and auxiliary assumptions, so a failure never says which part to blame. The wobble in Uranus's orbit could have refuted Newton. Astronomers blamed an unseen planet and found Neptune. The same move applied to Mercury's perihelion, and that planet was never there. Imre Lakatos rebuilt the idea around research programmes, judged over time by whether they keep predicting new things.
