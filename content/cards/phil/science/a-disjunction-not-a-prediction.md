# A simulation argument with three exits

Nick Bostrom's 2003 argument does not identify our world as a simulation. It says at least one of three claims holds: humanity is very likely to die out before a posthuman stage; posthuman civilizations are extremely unlikely to run many simulations of their ancestors; or we are almost certainly simulated. If advanced civilizations made vast numbers of conscious simulated people, the simulated observers could greatly outnumber the original ones. But that conditional depends on assumptions about computing, consciousness and how to reason about one's own place among observers. Bostrom's conclusion is the **disjunction**, not its third branch. Which exit would you challenge first?

- [x] That at least one of three propositions is true — the argument rules out holding all three negations together, and stops there.
- [ ] That posthuman civilisations are impossible — that is the first disjunct, which the argument neither asserts nor denies.
