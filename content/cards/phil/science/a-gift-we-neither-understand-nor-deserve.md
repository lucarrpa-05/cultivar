# Why does invented mathematics fit the world?

In 1960 physicist Eugene Wigner called mathematics' fit with physical laws “a wonderful gift which we neither understand nor deserve.” Calculus was developed as a mathematical tool and became indispensable in physics; complex numbers, once puzzling algebraic objects, became central to quantum theory. Why should ideas explored for mathematical reasons describe experiments so well? A Platonist may say mathematics discovers structures already there. A formalist may ask why some rule-governed symbol systems map onto nature. Wigner did not resolve the puzzle, and these are neither the only views nor automatic solutions. The next time an equation predicts a measurement, notice the extra question hidden inside the success.
