# The machine on Mars builds your copy. Do you get in?

Derek Parfit imagines a machine that scans you on Earth, destroys your body, and builds a physically exact counterpart on Mars. The person there remembers entering the machine. Would that person be you? Now remove the destruction step: one person walks out on Earth while another walks out on Mars, both with your memories. They cannot both be numerically identical to the one person who entered, yet each has the same psychological connection to that earlier life. Parfit uses the branch to ask whether identity is what matters for survival. Before opening the answer, decide which loss worries you: your body, your memories, or being the *only* continuer.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What does Parfit conclude from the branching case?
A: Parfit argues that identity need not be what matters in survival. In the branching case, each continuer has psychological connectedness and continuity with the earlier person, though one person cannot be identical to both. This relation may preserve much of what we care about.
