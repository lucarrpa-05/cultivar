# Why does seeing red feel like anything?

Imagine neuroscientists mapping every circuit involved in seeing a tomato. They could explain colour discrimination, attention and speech. David Chalmers called these the “easy problems” in 1995: difficult work, but work about functions. His **hard problem** asks why performing those functions is accompanied by experience at all. Frank Jackson's earlier thought experiment makes the gap vivid. Mary knows every physical fact about colour vision but has lived only in a black-and-white room. When she first sees red, does she learn a fact? Philosophers disagree about the answer; Jackson himself later rejected his argument against physicalism. The question survives: what would count as explaining the feeling itself?
