# Someone just as careful as you disagrees

You calculate your share of a restaurant bill as \$43. Your friend gets \$45. You have checked each other's arithmetic for years and trust her judgment as much as your own. What should you believe now? On an **equal-weight** view of peer disagreement, her answer gives you reason to lower confidence in yours. That does *not* mean simply declaring the bill \$44: the view splits confidence in competing answers, not the numbers themselves. It also asks you not to discount her merely because she disagrees. Steadfast philosophers reply that the original evidence may still favor one calculation. Check the bill, of course. Before checking, how much should her disagreement move you?
