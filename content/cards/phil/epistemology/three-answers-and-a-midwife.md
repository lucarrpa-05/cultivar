# Three answers, and a midwife who delivers none of them

Socrates calls himself a midwife in Plato's *Theaetetus*: he helps others bring ideas into the world, then tests whether those ideas can survive. The young mathematician Theaetetus offers three answers to “What is knowledge?” Perception fails because judging what something *is* goes beyond sensing it. True belief fails because a persuaded juror may reach the right verdict without knowing why. True belief plus an account seems promising, but the dialogue tests several meanings of “account” and accepts none. Socrates leaves for his trial with the question still open. Later philosophers would return to a related idea: justified true belief. What, if anything, could still go wrong with that?
