# The test came back positive. Now what?

A condition affects 1 in 100 people. There is a screening test. If you have the condition, it flags you 90% of the time. If you do not, it still flags you 9% of the time.

You get screened, with no symptoms and no reason to suspect anything. The test is positive.

Before reading on, write down a number: what is the probability that you actually have the condition? Most people, including a famous share of doctors asked this in studies, answer somewhere between 70% and 90%.

Do not reach for the formula. Imagine 1,000 people walking through the door and just count them.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Out of 1,000 screened people, what share of the positives are real?
A: About 9%. Of 1,000 people, 10 have the condition and 9 of them test positive. Of the 990 healthy ones, 9% — about 89 people — also test positive. So roughly 98 positives, of which 9 are real: 9 in 98, near 9%. The test is accurate; the base rate is tiny, and the tiny base rate wins.
