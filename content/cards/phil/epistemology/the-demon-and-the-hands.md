# The demon, and the man who answered with his hands

Suppose every sight and sensation is supplied by a powerful deceiver. Descartes's 1641 thought experiment makes even ordinary knowledge look vulnerable: how could you tell, from inside the experience, that the world is real? In 1939 G. E. Moore raised one hand, then the other, saying “Here is one hand” and “here is another.” For Moore, knowing that he had hands was firmer than the skeptical premise that would deny it. The gestures do not show *why* the deceiver is impossible; they challenge which claim deserves our confidence. But what happens when someone just as careful as you sees the evidence differently?
