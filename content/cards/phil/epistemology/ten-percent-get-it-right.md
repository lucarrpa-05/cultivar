# The card you should turn over is red

Four cards show **3**, **8**, **blue**, and **red**. Each has a number on one face and a colour on the other. Test this rule: *if a number is even, the other face is blue*. Which cards must you turn? **8** could hide a non-blue face; **red** could hide an even number. Blue cannot disprove the rule. People often miss this abstract version of Wason's selection task, yet do better when the same logic becomes “If someone drinks alcohol, they must be over 18.” The contrast inspired a proposed explanation about detecting social-rule violations, though that explanation remains contested. Does context help you reason, or change what you think the rule asks?

## Recall
type: mcq
Q: Which two cards test the rule "if even, then blue"?
- [ ] 8 and blue — blue cannot refute the rule whatever is on its back, so turning it is wasted.
- [x] 8 and red — 8 might hide a non-blue back, and red might hide an even number; those are the only ways the rule can fail.
- [ ] 3 and blue — the rule says nothing about odd numbers, so 3 is irrelevant either way.
