# The sun has made you no promises

Why will the sun rise tomorrow? Because it always has. Fine — but that argument needs a bridge: the future resembles the past. Where did you get that? From the fact that, so far, the future has resembled the past. You have just used induction to justify induction.

David Hume laid this out in the *Treatise* of 1739 and again, more readably, in the *Enquiry* of 1748, and nobody has got around it since. Deduction cannot help: it is not contradictory to suppose the sun fails to rise. Experience cannot help either, because every appeal to experience already presupposes the uniformity in question.

Hume's own conclusion is stranger than scepticism. He does not tell you to stop expecting sunrise; he says the expectation was never the work of reason. It is custom — a habit of mind produced by repetition, the same mechanism a dog has. Reason discovers the gap and then watches you step over it anyway.

You might think the damage is confined to whether the future resembles the past. In 1955 Nelson Goodman showed it is worse: even *past* evidence does not tell you which regularity you have observed.
