# Three became two, and the centre-back was born

Before 1925 an attacker needed three opponents between him and the goal line. Defences had turned this into a weapon: two full-backs stepping up in unison, killing move after move before it started. It was legal, effective and unwatchable.

The law was changed from three to two. The effect was immediate and enormous. English league clubs scored 4,700 goals in 1,848 matches in 1924–25; the following season, over the same number of matches, they scored 6,373.

Defences had to reorganise or drown, and Herbert Chapman's Arsenal reorganised hardest. The centre-half, until then a roaming playmaker in the 2-3-5 pyramid, was pulled back between the full-backs to sit as a third defender: the "stopper". Two inside-forwards dropped to link play. Written out, the shape makes a W and an M. By the late 1930s most English clubs had adopted the WM, and it stayed the default for a generation.

The modern centre-back — the defender whose only job is defending — is not a football idea. It is an artefact of a rule change made to stop a loophole.
