# The footnotes were three times longer than the article

In 1843 Ada Lovelace translated Luigi Menabrea's account of Babbage's proposed Analytical Engine. Then she added seven notes, together much longer than the article. Note G lays out steps for calculating a Bernoulli number on a machine that had not been built. The published table contains an error; transcribed exactly, it would give the wrong result.

Calling Lovelace the first programmer is contested because Babbage had drafted earlier unpublished programs. Her published Notes still did something striking: they treated the Engine as a machine for manipulating symbols, not merely adding numbers. She also insisted that it could carry out instructions but could not originate ideas on its own.
