# A million words on alchemy, sold at auction

Newton wrote or copied roughly a million words about alchemy. Most of these manuscripts stayed unpublished after his death. In 1936 the Earl of Portsmouth sent 329 lots of Newton's papers to Sotheby's; over a third contained alchemical material.

John Maynard Keynes bought many of them, along with Newton's death mask. Reading the manuscripts led him to call Newton “the last of the magicians.” That line does not erase the *Principia*. It asks us to stop dividing Newton neatly into a modern scientist on one side and a secret hobbyist on the other. Alchemy occupied a serious part of his working life.

type: reveal
Q: Why did Keynes call Newton "the last of the magicians"?
A: Because the manuscripts he bought at the 1936 Sotheby's sale showed Newton spending enormous effort — around a million words — on alchemy and prophecy, pursued with the same seriousness as the *Principia*, not as a hobby separable from it.
