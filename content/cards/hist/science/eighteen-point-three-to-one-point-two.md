# One ward, one rule, a dramatic drop

At Vienna General Hospital, mothers died of puerperal fever far more often in the maternity clinic staffed by doctors and students than in the one staffed by midwives. The doctors also performed autopsies. Ignaz Semmelweis suspected material from cadavers reached patients on their hands.

In mid-May 1847 he ordered washing with chlorinated lime before examinations. Deaths in the First Clinic had reached 18.3% in April; the rate fell to 2.2% in June and 1.2% in July. That dramatic comparison helped him argue for handwashing, but he lacked a germ theory and faced resistance from colleagues. He died in 1865, before his explanation gained broad acceptance.
