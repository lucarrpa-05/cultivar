# Twenty-nine countries, fifty-four per cent of the world

Bandung, West Java, 18 to 24 April 1955. Twenty-nine Asian and African delegations, convened by Indonesia, Burma, India, Ceylon and Pakistan, representing about 1.5 billion people — 54% of the world's population. They agreed ten principles, among them non-intervention, racial equality and the refusal to let collective defence pacts serve great-power interests. Neither bloc had been invited. The Non-Aligned Movement followed at Belgrade in 1961.
