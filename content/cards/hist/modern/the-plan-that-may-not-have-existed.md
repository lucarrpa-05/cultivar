# The plan that may not have existed

The familiar story says Schlieffen left Germany a perfect 1905 blueprint: sweep through Belgium, defeat France, then turn east. His successor Moltke supposedly weakened it and lost the war. After German archives reopened, historian Terence Zuber argued that this tidy plan was largely a postwar construction by officers defending their reputations.

Other historians object. The 1905 memorandum exists, and they disagree about how it related to the multiple deployment plans Germany actually prepared. It even described a war against France alone, unlike the two-front war of 1914. The point is not that no plan existed; it is that the phrase “the Schlieffen Plan” can hide a contested chain of documents, decisions and later excuses.
