# He called five missiles a false alarm

On 26 September 1983, Soviet officer Stanislav Petrov saw a warning of one American missile launch, then four more. He reported a malfunction. A first strike would probably involve far more missiles, he reasoned, and the satellite system was new. Ground radar later found no corroboration.

The alarm came from a rare alignment of sunlight, high clouds and satellite orbits. Petrov could not know that when he made his call; he later said he was never certain the warning was false. The incident became public in 1998. His judgment mattered, but several further assessments stood between his report and any Soviet launch. The famous tale is strongest when it leaves that uncertainty in place.
