# A gold crash, or an ordinary fluctuation?

Mansa Musa of Mali travelled to Mecca in 1324–25 and distributed gold along the route. Later retellings say he wrecked Cairo's gold market. The near-contemporary writer al-Umari gives a narrower observation: a mithqal of gold had fetched 25 silver dirhams, then less than 22, a fall of more than twelve per cent. He says it stayed below that level for at least twelve years.

But the historian Warren Schultz argues that such a change was within ordinary fluctuations in Mamluk Egypt. We have a report of a price movement, not a controlled experiment proving one traveller caused a lasting crash. The story survives; the certainty about its economic scale does not.
