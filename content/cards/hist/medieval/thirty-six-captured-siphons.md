# Thirty-six captured flamethrowers, and none of them worked

Greek fire appears around 672, credited by the chronicler Theophanes to Kallinikos, and it helped Constantinople survive the Arab sieges of 674–678 and 717–718. It burned on water and was sprayed from bronze tubes on a ship's prow.

The full method was guarded and compartmentalised: operators knew only parts of the system. When Bulgarians seized 36 siphons and a supply of the substance in 814, they could not make use of them. Scholars generally suspect a petroleum-based mixture, but its exact composition remains unknown.
