# Write it down twice and the errors announce themselves

In 1494 the Franciscan Luca Pacioli published a mathematics book in Venice with instructions for a method merchants already used: double-entry bookkeeping. Each transaction appears as a debit in one account and a credit in another. If the totals do not match, an entry is missing or wrong somewhere—though matching totals cannot catch every mistake.

The paired entries also let a merchant track assets and debts, not merely cash received and spent. That makes it possible to distinguish profit from money currently in the till. Pacioli did not invent the method. Printing a clear account of it helped spread a practical tool beyond the merchants who had learned it by apprenticeship.
