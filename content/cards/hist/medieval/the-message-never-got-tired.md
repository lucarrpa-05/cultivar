# The message never got tired, only the horses did

Across the Mongol empire stood relay stations, the *yam*, about twenty-five miles apart by Marco Polo's count. A courier carrying a *paiza*, an engraved tablet of authority, swapped his spent horse for a rested one at each station and rode on. Polo claims express riders made two hundred to two hundred and fifty miles in a day. That is a traveller's number, not a ledger's, so read it as an order of magnitude. The idea, though, is exact: the empire's news outran its armies.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What is the trick that let a Mongol dispatch sustain a speed no single rider could?
A: Only the horse is relieved, not the message. Relay stations roughly every twenty-five miles supplied a fresh mount, so the dispatch travelled at gallop pace continuously instead of decaying to the endurance of one animal.
