# Athens thought elections were the aristocratic option

Most Athenian public posts were filled by lot. The Boule of five hundred was drawn each year; from the fourth century BCE juries were allotted on the morning of the trial by a slotted stone machine, the *kleroterion*, built specifically to make rigging hard. Voting was kept for jobs where competence obviously mattered, such as the ten generals. Aristotle records the received view — lot is democratic, election oligarchic — because elections favour the famous and the rich, and a lottery cannot be canvassed.
