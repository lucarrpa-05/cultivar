# The first strike on record was about late pay

In year 29 of Ramesses III, around 1156 BCE, the artisans building royal tombs at Deir el-Medina stopped work. Their pay came as grain rations, and eighteen days into the month those rations had not arrived. The workers protested that they were hungry and had to buy their own wheat. Officials supplied food, and the crew returned; later stoppages followed. This is the earliest labour strike preserved in written records. The scene is familiar: skilled workers demanding the wages they had already earned.

Q: Who went on strike at Deir el-Medina, and over what?
A: The skilled craftsmen who built the royal tombs, in year 29 of Ramesses III (about 1156 BC). They were paid in grain rations, the rations did not arrive, and they refused to work until the shortfall was made good.
