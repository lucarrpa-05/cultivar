# The emperor who published his ethics in Greek

Ashoka ruled roughly 268 to 232 BCE and left inscriptions on rocks and pillars across his empire. Most used Prakrit; some in the northwest used Greek or Aramaic, languages familiar there. The Brahmi and Kharoshthi scripts later fell out of use, though Greek did not. James Prinsep's decipherment, published in 1837–38, made the Prakrit edicts readable again. A ruler had tried to address different subjects in languages they could understand; centuries later, recovering the writing was needed to hear him.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Why are Ashoka's edicts written in Greek and Aramaic as well as Prakrit?
A: Because they were meant to be read by the people living there. In the empire's northwest, Greek and Aramaic were the working languages, so the same royal message was cut in all three.
