# The legal width of a cart

After unifying China in 221 BCE, the Qin standardized writing, weights, measures and the distance between carriage wheels. An early historian listed axle width beside law and script among the first emperor's achievements. The practical appeal is easy to picture: carts built to the same gauge can use the same road ruts. But that explanation is an inference from the reform, not proof that every imperial road had one fixed track. Li Si helped promulgate the script standard. Governing conquered regions meant making everyday systems work together.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Why would a state legislate the length of a chariot axle?
A: Because unconnected kingdoms had incompatible vehicles and roadways. Fixing the axle length is what lets a cart loaded in one conquered state travel and trade in another — the same logic as a standard gauge or a standard file format.
