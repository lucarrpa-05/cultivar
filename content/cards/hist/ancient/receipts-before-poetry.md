# Writing was invented to settle accounts

The earliest tablets from Uruk, roughly 3300 BCE, carry no gods and no kings. They carry quantities: barley, beer, sheep, textiles, and the names of whoever was owed them. Literature turns up centuries later. Denise Schmandt-Besserat traced the signs back further, to clay tokens sealed inside clay envelopes — accounting that existed before writing did, though her reconstruction is widely contested. Humanity learned to write because it could not remember who owed what.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What kind of text dominates the earliest cuneiform tablets?
A: Administrative accounts — rations, debts and deliveries of barley, beer and livestock. Narrative and religious texts appear only centuries afterwards.
