# There is no AI regulator, and that is the point

People picture AI policy as one agency with one rulebook. Nowhere has that. Structurally there are four layers, and most of the weight sits on the first.

**Existing law already applies.** A discriminatory hiring model breaks employment law. A model that defames you is covered by defamation law. A diagnostic device is a medical device whether or not it contains a neural network. No new statute required, and this layer is doing most of the work right now.

**Sectoral regulators** interpret that law for their patch — medicines, finance, aviation — which is why the same model faces heavy duties in a hospital and none in a video game.

**Horizontal rules** cut across sectors: risk tiers, transparency duties, incident reporting, pre-deployment evaluation. The EU's AI Act is the main worked example.

**Supply-chain measures** act on the inputs rather than the model — export controls on chips, government procurement rules, cloud reporting requirements.

So when someone asks whether AI should be regulated, the useful reply is: which layer? They have different enforcers, different standards of evidence, and wildly different speeds.
