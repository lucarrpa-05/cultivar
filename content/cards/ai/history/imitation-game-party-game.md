# The Turing test began as a game about telling men from women

Turing's 1950 paper opens with a game that has no machine in it. Three people: a man (A), a woman (B), and an interrogator (C) who sees neither and communicates by note. C must work out which is which. A's job is to deceive; B's is to help. Turing even supplies a sample question: "Will X please tell me the length of his or her hair?"

Only then comes the swerve. "What will happen when a machine takes the part of A in this game?"

So the original test is not "can a machine pass for a human". It is: can a machine deceive as well as a man pretending to be a woman? Imitation under an assumed identity was in the design from the first paragraph.

He also set a deadline. In about fifty years, he wrote, a computer with storage of about $10^9$ would play well enough that an average interrogator would have no more than a 70 per cent chance of identifying correctly after five minutes. Read that again: he predicted a machine that fools you three times in ten. Not a pass. A respectable failure.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: In Turing's original setup, what is the machine replacing?
A: Player A — the man who is trying to convince the interrogator that he is the woman. The test was built on imitation under a false identity, not on "acting human" in general.
