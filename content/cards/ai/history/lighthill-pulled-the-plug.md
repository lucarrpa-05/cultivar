# One report, and British AI lost its funding

The Science Research Council wanted an outsider's opinion, so it asked Sir James Lighthill — an eminent fluid dynamicist with no stake in the field. His 1973 survey, *Artificial Intelligence: A General Survey*, divided the subject into three: A for advanced automation, C for computer-based studies of the nervous system, and B for the "bridge" between them, the robotics work that was supposed to connect the two. His verdict on all of it:

> In no part of the field have the discoveries made so far produced the major impact that was then promised.

Category B, he argued, had no results of its own and survived by borrowing justification from A and C. The technical charge was the combinatorial explosion: methods that shine on toy problems grow exponentially with the size of the problem, so success on a blocks-world demo predicts nothing about the real one.

On 9 May 1973 the BBC televised a debate at the Royal Institution — Lighthill against Donald Michie, John McCarthy and Richard Gregory. Whoever won the evening lost the argument that counted: the report became the basis for withdrawing government support from AI at most British universities.
