# The bullet holes missing from the hangar

In 1943 Abraham Wald studied damage on aircraft that returned from combat. The visible holes showed where surviving planes had been hit. They could not directly show where hits made a plane fail to return. Wald developed a method for estimating vulnerability from this selected sample, so armor could be placed where it would matter most. The neat slogan “armor the places without holes” captures the insight, but it is a later retelling, not a documented quotation from him. The general habit is sharper than the slogan: before trusting a dataset, ask what had to happen for an observation to enter it.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What sampling problem did Wald's 1943 memoranda solve?
A: Returning aircraft are selected survivors, so their damage pattern under-represents potentially fatal hit locations. Wald used the selected data to estimate vulnerability and inform armor placement.
