# Why a skate glides even when pressure cannot melt ice

A familiar account says the blade's pressure melts the ice beneath it. Pressure can lower ice's melting point, but too little to explain skating on much colder ice. Hockey surfaces are commonly kept several degrees below zero. A thin, mobile surface layer and heat generated as a moving blade rubs the ice are among the mechanisms researchers study. Their contributions change with temperature, speed and load; there is no single effortless explanation for every slippery surface. The useful correction is narrower than “pressure has no effect.” Pressure alone cannot make the water film that the old textbook story needs under ordinary skating conditions.
