# Ten thousand letters said she was wrong about the goats

On 9 September 1990, Marilyn vos Savant answered the three-door puzzle in her *Parade* column: switch, and you win two times in three. About 10,000 readers wrote in, close to 1,000 of them PhDs, most telling her she was wrong. Paul Erdős stayed unconvinced until he saw a computer simulation.

In July 1991 *The New York Times* asked Monty Hall himself. The experts' one-in-two, he said, was "the same assumption contestants would make on the show" once a losing door was open. Then the catch: on his show he did not have to offer the switch, and if the host has that choice, "beware."

Two-thirds is a fact about the host's rules, not about the doors.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Under what rule is switching worth two-thirds, according to the puzzle and to Monty Hall himself?
A: The host must always open a goat door and always offer the switch. If he can choose when to offer it, all bets are off; if he opens a door at random and happens to show a goat, switching wins only half the time.
