# Three switches, one visit to the bulb

Outside a closed room are three switches. Exactly one controls an incandescent bulb inside; the other two do nothing. The bulb starts off. You may flip switches as often as you like, but enter the room only once. When you enter, you must know which switch controls it.

Looking at the bulb gives only “on” or “off,” apparently too little for three choices. But an incandescent bulb retains heat after being switched off. You can arrange for one switch to leave the bulb lit, another to leave it dark but warm, and the third to leave it dark and cold. What sequence creates those three distinguishable outcomes?

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What do you do before entering, and what do the three observations mean?
A: Turn switch A on for several minutes, then off. Turn B on and enter promptly. Lit means B; dark but warm means A; dark and cold means C. This depends on a bulb that becomes warm while lit and stays warm briefly.
