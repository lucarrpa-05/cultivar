# Forty-three quintillion positions; twenty turns to solve

A standard Rubik's Cube has 43,252,003,274,489,856,000 reachable arrangements. Corners and edges move, but parity and orientation constrain them. Despite that enormous state space, a computer-assisted proof completed in 2010 showed that every reachable position can be solved in at most **20 face turns**, counting a half-turn as one move. Some positions need all twenty, so the bound is exact. Counting quarter-turns only, the maximum is 26. The achievement was a bound on the *worst possible* scramble, not just a short solution to one puzzle.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What does "God's number is 20" mean for a Rubik's cube?
A: That the cube's state graph has diameter 20 in the half-turn metric: no scramble, however evil, is more than twenty face turns from solved. Rokicki, Kociemba, Davidson and Dethridge proved it by computer in 2010; the quarter-turn answer, 26, followed in 2014.
