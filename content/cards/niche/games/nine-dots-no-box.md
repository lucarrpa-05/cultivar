# Nine dots, four strokes, one invented boundary

Put nine dots on paper in a three-by-three square. Connect all nine with four straight, continuous line segments. You cannot lift your pen or retrace a segment. The lines may cross, and a segment may pass through more than two dots.

Most attempts turn at an outside dot, as if the square of dots were a wall. But the drawing has no border. Let your first line continue *past* the last dot. From there a diagonal can catch two dots that seemed impossible to reach together. The hard part is noticing a rule you supplied yourself and quietly removing it.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What hidden assumption makes the four-line solution seem impossible?
A: That every turn must lie inside the square of dots. Start at the top-left dot, draw right through the top row and beyond; diagonal down-left through middle-right and bottom-middle to below bottom-left; straight up through the left column; then diagonal down-right through the center and bottom-right.
