# One photograph, three colours of dress

The dress was blue and black. Yet when its photograph spread online in 2015, many viewers saw white and gold, and some saw blue and brown. In a study of about 1,400 people, the three answers drew roughly 57%, 30% and 11%. Your eyes receive light reflected from cloth *and* illumination; your visual system estimates the lighting to infer the cloth's colour. This photograph leaves the lighting ambiguous, so different estimates can lead to different experiences even on the same screen. Neither group is pretending. The disagreement is a glimpse of the guesswork that ordinarily makes a white shirt look white under changing light.
