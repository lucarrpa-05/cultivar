# There is no English word for the best part of lunch

*Sobremesa*: the stretch of time that starts when dessert is served and ends whenever the conversation does. Nobody stands up. Coffee appears, maybe an aguardiente. The meal is over and the table is not.

"Untranslatable" is an overstatement — that paragraph just translated it. What English lacks is a *single word*, a different and more interesting claim: languages hand short labels to whatever their speakers do often enough to need one.

The gap is not about concepts. It is about how long you sit there.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What does a so-called "untranslatable" word usually mean?
A: Not that the idea cannot be expressed — almost anything can be paraphrased — but that the other language has no *single word* for it. Short labels get minted for what a culture does often, so lexical gaps map habits rather than thoughts.
