# Your coffee is a plant's chemical weapon, badly aimed

Caffeine is a defence molecule. It protects the coffee plant against herbivores, and it leaches into the soil where it suppresses the germination of competing seeds — the plant poisons its own neighbourhood to keep the space. Some plants also put small doses in their nectar, where it encourages the bees rather than repelling them.

We drink it. And here is the part that changes how you think about a cup: caffeine gives you nothing.

All day, your brain accumulates adenosine, a by-product that binds to receptors and produces the sensation of tiredness. Caffeine is shaped enough like adenosine to occupy those receptors without activating them. It does not add energy, or alertness, or capability. It sits in the doorway so the tiredness signal cannot get in.

The adenosine is still arriving the whole time. When the caffeine clears, all of it lands at once — which is the usual explanation for the afternoon crash.
