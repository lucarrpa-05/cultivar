# The Andes kept thousands of potatoes. Ireland had fewer bets.

The Andean region still holds thousands of potato varieties, with different tolerances to cold and disease. In nineteenth-century Ireland, many poor households depended heavily on one productive variety, the Lumper. When *Phytophthora infestans* blight struck in 1845, that dependence magnified the loss. Genetic diversity alone would not explain the Great Famine: unequal landholding, poverty and political decisions shaped who starved and who could leave. But the crop contrast is a clear lesson in correlated risk. A field planted with similar potatoes may look efficient in a good year; when they share a weakness, one pathogen can threaten every household relying on it.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Why is keeping thousands of potato varieties a rational strategy rather than inefficiency?
A: Varieties can differ in resistance to frost and pathogens, so a diverse crop spreads risk. Dependence on the Lumper increased Irish vulnerability to blight, although poverty and land policy also shaped the famine.
