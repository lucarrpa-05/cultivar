# The browning that needs sugar *and* protein

The smell of toasted bread or seared meat owes much to the Maillard reaction. A reducing sugar reacts with amino groups in food, setting off many further reactions that create aromas and brown compounds. Caramelization, by contrast, begins with sugars alone. Louis-Camille Maillard described the sugar–amino-acid chemistry in 1912; John Hodge later mapped major steps in its mechanism. This is why a cook often dries food before browning it: while water is evaporating, the surface struggles to get hot enough for the rapid reactions that build a crust. Crowding a pan slows that drying. Browning is chemistry, but it is also a problem of moisture and heat.
