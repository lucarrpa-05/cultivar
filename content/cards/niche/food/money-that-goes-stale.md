# Cacao beans were small change you could eat

In parts of Mesoamerica, cacao beans served as small change as well as an ingredient for drink. A 1545 price list from Tlaxcala gives the scale: a large tomato cost one bean; a small rabbit, dozens. The beans were countable and valued, but perishable. Fraud was possible too: accounts describe counterfeit beans filled or imitated with cheaper materials. Money need not be metal or paper. It needs people willing to accept it in trade, and enough trust that each bean is what it appears to be.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What made cacao beans work as currency, and what made them fail?
A: Beans were portable, countable and valued in trade, so they could serve as small change. But they could spoil or be faked, making quality and trust a continuing problem.
