# The onion makes its irritant only after you cut it

An intact onion keeps key chemicals apart inside its cells. Your knife breaks those compartments. Alliinase enzymes produce sulfenic acids, and a separate enzyme identified in 2002 turns one into the volatile compound that reaches your eyes: syn-propanethial S-oxide. Tears respond to that irritant, not to an acid waiting in the whole bulb. The onion is a chemical machine triggered by damage. Chilling or cutting under water can reduce how much airborne irritant reaches you, though neither changes the reaction itself.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Why does an onion only make you cry once it is cut?
A: Because the irritant does not exist in the intact bulb. Cutting ruptures cells, letting alliinase enzymes reach their substrates to make sulfenic acids; lachrymatory-factor synthase then converts one into the volatile syn-propanethial S-oxide. It is a defence assembled on damage.
