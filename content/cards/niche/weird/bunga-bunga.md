# Virginia Woolf, in a false beard, inspecting the Royal Navy

On 7 February 1910, six people in turbans and darkened skin were received aboard HMS *Dreadnought*, the most advanced and most secret warship in the world, as a delegation of Abyssinian royalty. The Royal Navy gave them a full ceremonial reception.

The visitors were Horace de Vere Cole and friends, among them the 28-year-old Virginia Stephen — later Virginia Woolf — in a false beard. Their costumes came from a theatrical outfitter. Their language was gibberish improvised from scraps of Latin and Greek. They asked for prayer mats and tried to award fake military honours to the officers, who accepted them.

The press reported that the delegation kept exclaiming *bunga bunga*. The phrase stuck to the ship for years; when *Dreadnought* rammed a German submarine in 1915, someone sent a congratulatory telegram reading only: BUNGA BUNGA.

A hoax survives on confidence, not on plausibility. Nobody checked, because nobody could imagine anyone daring.
