# The Poincaré conjecture was solved on a preprint server

No journal. No press conference. Between November 2002 and July 2003, Grigori Perelman posted three preprints to arXiv, and buried inside them was the proof of the Poincaré conjecture — open since 1904, and the only one of the seven Millennium Problems that has been solved.

Then he began refusing things. He resigned from the Steklov Institute in December 2005. In 2006 he was awarded the Fields Medal and declined it, the only person ever to do so. In March 2010 the Clay Mathematics Institute awarded him its million-dollar prize; on 1 July he turned that down too.

His stated reason was not modesty. It was a judgement about the community: he considered the decision unjust because it did not recognise Richard Hamilton, whose Ricci flow programme the proof completes, and he had lost patience with a field whose ethical standards he thought had slipped.

Whatever you make of the refusals, one consequence is concrete. The single most valuable piece of mathematics of the century sits on a public server, free, with no publisher between it and you.
