# The forgery of a forgery outsold the original

On 16 October 1869, well-diggers behind a barn in Cardiff, New York, hit a three-metre stone man weighing nearly 1,400 kg. Crowds came at fifty cents a head, three to five hundred a day, to see a petrified biblical giant.

George Hull had carved it from Iowa gypsum and buried it the year before, at a cost of about \$2,600. He sold his share for \$23,000 — close to \$600,000 today — after losing an argument at a Methodist revival meeting about whether giants had really walked the Earth.

Then the part nobody plans. P. T. Barnum offered \$50,000, was refused, and simply had a copy made in plaster. He displayed the copy, in New York, while the original was also on show in New York. It is here that a rival exhibitor, David Hannum, is said to have complained that there's a sucker born every minute — a line that has been attached to Barnum ever since, and that Barnum appears not to have said.

Two men made fortunes from the same lie, and only one of them had to dig a hole.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Who is actually credited with saying "there's a sucker born every minute", and about what?
A: David Hannum, a rival showman, reportedly said it about the crowds paying to see *Barnum's plaster copy* of the Cardiff Giant — a fake of a fake. The line is almost universally misattributed to Barnum himself.
