# Nobody burned the Library of Alexandria

The story wants a villain. Caesar in 48 BC, or a Christian mob in 391, or the Arab conquest of 642 — pick your culprit and the greatest collection of antiquity goes up in one afternoon, taking the ancient world's knowledge with it.

The evidence does not cooperate. Caesar's fire seems to have reached warehouses near the docks, not the main collection: the Library is attested afterwards. The Serapeum, destroyed in 391, may well have held no significant books by then. The 642 story appears in sources written centuries later and is treated with deep suspicion.

What actually happened is duller and much worse. In 145 BC Ptolemy VIII expelled foreign scholars from Alexandria and they scattered across the eastern Mediterranean. The institution's prestige went with them. Funding declined. Appointments became political. The city was fought over repeatedly — Aurelian's forces wrecked the very quarter where the Library stood in the 270s AD.

So there was no afternoon. There were three hundred years of nobody quite paying for it any more. Which is the version worth remembering, because it is the version that can happen to you.
