# The 1904 marathon had a driver and a poisoned winner

At the St. Louis Olympics, runners started a dusty marathon in heat around 32 °C with little water. Fred Lorz reached the stadium first after riding part of the course in a car; officials disqualified him. Thomas Hicks won, barely standing after his handlers gave him strychnine, brandy and egg whites, substances then used as stimulants. Cuba's Andarín Carvajal stopped for food, suffered cramps and still finished fourth. Accounts differ on some colourful details, but the car ride and dangerous dosing are documented. The race shows how rough early Olympic practices could be; calling every bizarre tactic “not cheating” would miss that Lorz was in fact punished.
