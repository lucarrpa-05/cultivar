# Why Pinzón called an ocean mouth the “Sweet Sea”

Near the Amazon's mouth in 1500, Vicente Yáñez Pinzón encountered river water and named the area *Mar Dulce*, the Sweet Sea. The Amazon's enormous discharge forms a surface plume with lower salinity than nearby Atlantic water. Its reach varies with season and currents. “Freshwater plume” does not mean every distant patch is safe to drink: river water mixes with seawater as it travels. The surprise is that a river can change the sea beyond its banks, enough for sailors to notice without seeing the immense watershed behind it.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Why did Pinzón describe the Amazon’s mouth as a “Sweet Sea”?
A: The Amazon sends so much water into the Atlantic that a lower-salinity surface plume forms near its mouth. Pinzón’s “Mar Dulce” refers to that striking encounter; farther offshore, the plume mixes with seawater and is not necessarily drinkable.
