# In Bogotá, boiling water is not hot enough

The city sits at about 2,625 m, Monserrate another 500 m above it. Thinner air means water boils sooner: tables give 93.4 °C at 2,000 m and 90.0 °C at 3,000 m, leaving Bogotá near 91–92 °C.

Boiling is a ceiling, not a setting. A pot cannot get hotter than its boiling point, so beans that soften in forty minutes at sea level take far longer, and extra flame does nothing. Hence the pressure cooker: serious Andean equipment, not a gadget.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Why does turning up the heat not fix slow cooking at altitude?
A: Because the water's temperature is pinned at its boiling point, which altitude has already lowered to about 91 °C. More flame boils it away faster; it does not make it hotter. Raising the pressure — a pressure cooker — is the only way to raise the ceiling.
