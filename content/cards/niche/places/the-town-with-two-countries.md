# A border that goes through a front door, and then a shed

Baarle is one town on the Dutch–Belgian frontier and two municipalities, and the map of it looks like someone spilled it. Baarle-Hertog, the Belgian side, consists of 26 separate parcels, 22 of them sitting entirely inside the Netherlands. Inside those Belgian islands there are seven Dutch islands — counter-enclaves — six of them within a single Belgian parcel.

Nobody designed this. It is sediment: centuries of medieval land deals, swaps and sales between the Lords of Breda and the Dukes of Brabant, each transaction recorded plot by plot, never tidied. When the Netherlands and Belgium finally drew a national border in the Treaty of Maastricht of 1843, they ratified the mess rather than rationalise it.

So the line runs through a furniture showroom, a shed, a barn and the loading dock of a liquor store. One house has the border through the middle of its front door and carries two addresses: Loveren 2, Baarle-Hertog and Loveren 19, Baarle-Nassau.

It is what a border looks like when it is inherited instead of drawn.
