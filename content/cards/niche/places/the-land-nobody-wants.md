# The only habitable place on Earth that nobody claims

Britain drew the Egypt–Sudan border twice. The 1899 line cut straight along the 22nd parallel; the 1902 line followed where the tribes grazed. They swap two areas: the Hala'ib Triangle on the Red Sea, and Bir Tawil, 2,060 km² of desert.

Egypt insists on the 1899 line, Sudan on the 1902 one. Each version hands its claimant Hala'ib — and hands Bir Tawil to the other side. Claiming Bir Tawil would mean conceding Hala'ib, so nobody does, and it stays the one habitable place on Earth outside every state.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Why would claiming Bir Tawil be a bad move for either Egypt or Sudan?
A: Because the two claims are a package. Each country's preferred border line simultaneously gives it the valuable Hala'ib Triangle and gives Bir Tawil away. Taking Bir Tawil means adopting the other country's line, which surrenders Hala'ib.
