# How many Greenlands fit inside Africa?

Picture the world map that hung in your classroom. Greenland is an enormous white slab across the top, comfortably the size of the African continent below it.

Now commit to a number. Africa's area divided by Greenland's: is it about 1? About 3? About 8? Bigger?

While you decide, the politics. In 1855 the Scottish clergyman James Gall described an equal-area projection that nobody noticed. In 1967 the German historian Arno Peters independently arrived at essentially the same map and, from 1973, promoted it hard as the anti-colonial correction to Mercator — the map that finally gives the global South its true size.

Cartographers were furious, partly because Peters claimed properties his map did not have, and partly because equal-area projections had been sitting on the shelf for a century. The fight got so tiresome that in 1989 seven North American geographic organisations adopted a joint resolution asking everyone to stop using *any* rectangular world map.

Both sides were right about something. Your number first.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: How many times larger than Greenland is Africa in reality?
A: About fourteen. Greenland is roughly 2.2 million km², Africa roughly 30 million. On Mercator they look comparable because Greenland sits almost entirely above 60° N, where the projection multiplies area by four or more, while Africa sits on the equator, where it multiplies by one.
