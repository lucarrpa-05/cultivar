# Same ballots, three rules, three winners

Twenty-one voters hand in these rankings, and nobody is lying:

- 8 of them rank A first, then B, then C
- 7 of them rank C first, then B, then A
- 6 of them rank B first, then C, then A

Before you read on, pick your winner. Then work out three answers. Who wins under plurality, counting only first preferences? Who wins a two-round runoff between the top two? And who beats *both* rivals in a head-to-head majority vote?

No ballot changes. No voter lies. Only the rule changes. Whichever answer you like best, notice what you just did: you chose a winner by choosing a procedure. Committees, primaries and constitutions make that choice for you, usually without mentioning it.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Under plurality, under a runoff, and under head-to-head majority, who wins?
A: Plurality: A, with 8 first preferences against C's 7 and B's 6. Runoff: A and C advance, B's six voters prefer C, so C wins 13 to 8. Head-to-head: B beats A 13 to 8 and beats C 14 to 7, so B is the Condorcet winner — and B wins under neither of the other two rules.
