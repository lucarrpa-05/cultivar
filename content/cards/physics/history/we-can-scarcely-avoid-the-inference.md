# The sentence where optics stopped being its own subject

Maxwell built a mechanical model of the electromagnetic field, computed the speed of waves in it from two constants measured with coils and jars, and got a number optics had already measured by other means.

In *On Physical Lines of Force* (1862): "we can scarcely avoid the inference that light consists in the transverse undulations of the same medium which is the cause of electric and magnetic phenomena."

Two sciences merged in one careful sentence, on the strength of an arithmetic coincidence he refused to treat as one.
