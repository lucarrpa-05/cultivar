# The year the patent clerk had four ideas

In 1905 Albert Einstein was 26, a technical expert third class at the Swiss patent office in Bern, examining electrical devices. He had graduated in 1900 and failed to get any academic post; a friend's father found him the patent job. He did physics in the evenings.

That year he published four papers in *Annalen der Physik*.

March: light comes in quanta, which explains the photoelectric effect. This is the one that won him the Nobel Prize in 1921, and the one he himself called revolutionary.

May: the jittering of tiny grains suspended in water — what Robert Brown had watched under a microscope in 1827 — is caused by molecular collisions, with a quantitative prediction for how far a grain wanders. It convinced the remaining sceptics that atoms exist, and it is his most cited paper.

June: on the electrodynamics of moving bodies — special relativity — written without a single citation.

September: a three-page addendum asking whether inertia depends on energy content. That one contains $E=mc^2$.

He stayed at the patent office until 1909.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Which of Einstein's 1905 papers won him the Nobel Prize, and which convinced physicists that atoms are real?
A: The Nobel (1921) was for the photoelectric effect — the March paper on light quanta — not for relativity, which remained contentious. The May paper on Brownian motion gave a testable quantitative prediction for the displacement of suspended particles; Perrin's experiments confirmed it and ended serious doubt about the existence of atoms.
