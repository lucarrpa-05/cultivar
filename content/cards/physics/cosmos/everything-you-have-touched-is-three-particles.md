# Everything you have ever touched is three particles

Your hands, this screen, the air, the Andes, the Sun: up quarks, down quarks, electrons. Three protons' worth of ingredients. Two ups and a down make a proton, two downs and an up make a neutron, electrons do the chemistry. Add photons so you can see it and the inventory of ordinary experience is complete.

The Standard Model has seventeen particles, and the surplus is the strange part. There is a second copy of those building blocks — heavier, unstable, otherwise identical — and then a third copy, heavier still. Nothing needs them. When the muon turned up in cosmic rays, a heavier twin of the electron with no job to do, I. I. Rabi is remembered for asking who ordered that.

Nobody has answered. Three generations, spanning at least eleven orders of magnitude in mass from the neutrinos to the top quark, and the theory that describes them cannot predict a single one of those masses.

The Standard Model is the most successful theory in physics and an unfinished sentence.

## Rigor

Content: three generations of $(u,d)$-type quarks and $(e,\nu_e)$-type leptons; twelve gauge bosons for the group $SU(3)_C\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$ — eight gluons, $W^{\pm}$, $Z^0$, photon — and one Higgs scalar. The headline "seventeen" counts species rather than states: the eight gluons count once between them, as do $W^+$ and $W^-$.

The forces are not extra assumptions. Demand that the Lagrangian be invariant under *local* transformations of that group, and the gauge fields are forced on you, along with their couplings to matter — the same trade the group-theory card described, symmetry in, structure out.

The embarrassment is the parameter count. Roughly nineteen numbers must be measured and inserted by hand: nine charged-fermion masses, three quark mixing angles plus a CP-violating phase, three gauge couplings, two Higgs parameters, and the strong CP angle. Neutrino masses and mixings add about seven more. None is predicted; the electron-to-top mass ratio alone spans five orders of magnitude with no explanation.

And the model is knowingly incomplete: it excludes gravity, provides no dark-matter candidate, and gives no mechanism for the matter–antimatter asymmetry that lets any of this exist. It has nonetheless survived every collider test attempted, which is the frustrating part.
