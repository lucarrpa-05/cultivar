# Yukawa weighed a particle nobody had seen

In 1935 Hideki Yukawa predicted a particle's mass from the size of a nucleus. His rule: forces travel by messenger, and a heavy messenger can't go far. The nuclear force dies within a nucleus's width, so its messenger should weigh about 200 times an electron.

In 1936 cosmic rays delivered one of nearly that mass. Wrong one: the muon, which barely touched nuclei. The real messenger, the pion, turned up in 1947 in photographic plates exposed on mountaintops, among them 5,200 metres up Chacaltaya, Bolivia, where a 22-year-old Brazilian, César Lattes, carried them.

Yukawa and Cecil Powell, the group's leader, got Nobels. Lattes, first author of the discovery paper, didn't. Brazil named its database of researchers' CVs after him.
