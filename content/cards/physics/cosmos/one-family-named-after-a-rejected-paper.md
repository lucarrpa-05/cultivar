# One family of particles is named after a man a journal turned down

In 1924 Satyendra Nath Bose, a reader at the new University of Dhaka with no doctorate, mailed a short paper to Einstein. The *Philosophical Magazine* had already turned it down. Einstein translated it into German himself and got it published.

Bose had counted particles of light as if no two could ever be told apart. That counting has a consequence: such particles happily crowd into the same state. Laser light is that crowd.

Electrons obey the opposite rule, Pauli's: never two in one state. Every particle in the Standard Model is one kind or the other. The loners, called fermions (quarks, electrons, neutrinos), make matter. The joiners, named bosons after Bose, are the force carriers and the Higgs.
