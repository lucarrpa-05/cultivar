# The Higgs gives you about one percent of your mass

On 4 July 2012, CERN announced a new particle at about 125 GeV, and the press explained that physicists had finally found the thing that gives everything its mass. That explanation is off by two orders of magnitude for you personally.

Almost all your mass is protons and neutrons. Almost none of *their* mass is the mass of the quarks inside them. Three quarks account for roughly one percent of a proton's mass; the other ninety-nine percent is the energy of the gluon field binding them and the kinetic energy of the quarks rattling around inside. By $E = mc^2$, that energy weighs. You are, to a very good approximation, made of confined energy.

What the Higgs field does do is give mass to the *elementary* particles — electrons, quarks, and crucially the $W$ and $Z$ bosons — and that matters enormously. Without it the electron would be massless, atoms would not exist, and there would be nothing to bind.

So the headline was wrong about the number and right about the stakes.

## Rigor

Start with a complex scalar field and a potential shaped like a sombrero:

$$V(\phi) = -\mu^2|\phi|^2 + \lambda|\phi|^4, \qquad \mu^2,\lambda > 0 .$$

The Lagrangian is symmetric under $\phi\to e^{i\theta}\phi$, but the minimum is not at $\phi=0$; it lies on a circle $|\phi| = v/\sqrt2$ with $v \approx 246$ GeV. The field must pick a point on that circle, so the *state* breaks a symmetry the *laws* retain — spontaneous symmetry breaking. Expand around the chosen minimum and the radial excitation is a massive scalar: the Higgs boson.

Gauge that symmetry and the would-be massless angular mode is eaten by the gauge field, which acquires $m_W = \tfrac12 g v$. Fermions get mass from Yukawa terms $-y_f\bar\psi\phi\psi \to -\tfrac{y_f v}{\sqrt2}\bar\psi\psi$, so $m_f = y_f v/\sqrt2$: the Higgs supplies the mechanism, but each $y_f$ is a free parameter, which is why the theory cannot predict the electron's mass.

The other ninety-nine percent is quantum chromodynamics. The proton's mass comes out of the QCD scale $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}} \sim 200$ MeV generated by the running of the strong coupling — mass from a dimensionless theory with no mass put in, which is arguably the more remarkable of the two mechanisms.

Q: Where does most of the mass of a proton come from?
- [ ] From the masses of its three quarks. — they contribute roughly one percent of the total.
- [x] From the energy of the gluon field and the quarks' motion inside it, converted by $E=mc^2$. — confinement energy is what a proton mostly weighs.
- [ ] From the Higgs field acting on the proton as a whole. — the Higgs couples to elementary particles, not to composites.
- [ ] From the binding energy of the electrons around the nucleus. — atomic binding energies are smaller by a factor of roughly a million.
