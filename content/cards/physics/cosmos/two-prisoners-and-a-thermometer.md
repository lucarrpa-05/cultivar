# Two prisoners and a thermometer

In 1914 James Chadwick showed that beta-decay electrons leave with a smear of energies, not one. Then war broke out, and he spent four years interned at Ruhleben, a racecourse near Berlin. In a horse stable he set up a lab with a fellow prisoner, Charles Ellis, a cadet caught on holiday, who left the army for physics.

Lise Meitner disagreed: the electrons must leave with one energy and lose some on the way out. In 1927 Ellis and William Wooster caught every electron in a calorimeter and measured the heat. It matched the smear's average, not its top. Meitner redid it more carefully and agreed.

The energy was really gone, unless something unseen was carrying it off.

## Recall
type: mcq
Q: What did the 1927 calorimeter settle?
- [ ] That electrons leave with one energy and lose some on the way out. — that was Meitner's view; if it were true, the heat would have matched the top of the smear.
- [x] That the energy is missing at the source: the heat matched the smear's average, so nothing was hiding as heat. — which left two options, give up energy conservation or find what was carrying it away.
- [ ] That energy conservation fails in nuclei. — the experiment showed the energy was missing, not why; Pauli's unseen particle was the other explanation.
