# The neutrino was first called the neutron

When Pauli proposed his invisible particle in 1930, the name was up for grabs: the heavy neutron had not been found. Physicists pictured a nucleus as protons with electrons packed in (nitrogen: fourteen protons, seven electrons), so beta decay looked like a nucleus letting an electron out.

Chadwick found the heavy neutron in 1932. In Rome, Fermi's circle nicknamed Pauli's particle the *neutrino*, "little neutral one", and Fermi's 1933–34 theory made the real shift: the electron was never inside. It is created when a neutron turns into a proton.
