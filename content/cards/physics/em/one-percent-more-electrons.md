# What if you had one percent more electrons than your friend?

Between two protons, the electric repulsion is about $10^{36}$ times the gravitational attraction. Thirty-six orders of magnitude. Electricity is not a slightly stronger force than gravity; it is a different kind of large.

So why does gravity run the universe while electricity mostly sits quiet? Because matter cancels itself out. Every atom carries exactly as many electrons as protons, and the two charges are equal to better than one part in $10^{20}$. Bulk matter is neutral, and neutral is boring.

Here is the estimate. You are made of roughly $2\times10^{28}$ electrons. Suppose you had one percent more of them than protons, and so did a friend standing a metre away. Both of you are now negative by the same amount.

How hard do you push each other apart? Guess before you look — and compare it to something you can picture, like the weight of a mountain.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: How large is the repulsion between two people a metre apart, each carrying one percent excess electrons?
A: Around $10^{25}$ newtons. One percent of $2\times10^{28}$ electrons is a charge of about $3\times10^{7}$ coulombs each, and $F = kq^2/r^2 \approx 9\times10^{9}\times(3\times10^{7})^2 \approx 10^{25}$ N. Mount Everest weighs about $10^{16}$ N, so this is a billion Everests — a good fraction of what it would take to lift the Earth. Feynman ran the same estimate in 1964 and landed in the same place. That is why matter bothers to be neutral.
