# Cosimo never said what he wanted. That was the strategy.

Padgett and Ansell reconstructed the marriage ties, business partnerships, loans and property dealings among Florence's elite families in the decades up to 1434, and then asked a question historians had been arguing about for five centuries: how did the Medici take a republic?

Not with money. Several rival families were wealthier. Not with office. The oligarchs held more seats.

What the Medici had was a shape. Their partners were drawn from blocs that had almost no ties to each other, so nearly every path through the elite network ran through them — high betweenness, and no competing broker. The rival party was dense and inward-looking: everybody connected to everybody, which sounds strong and means every member can talk to every other member without you.

Padgett and Ansell's term for what Cosimo did with that position is **robust action**: behaving in ways compatible with many different goals, never revealing which one is real. In a network where your allies cannot compare notes, ambiguity is not weakness. It is what lets you be several people at once.
