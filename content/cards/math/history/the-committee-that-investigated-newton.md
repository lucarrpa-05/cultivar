# Newton wrote the verdict on Newton

In 1711 Leibniz complained to the Royal Society after John Keill publicly implied he had borrowed calculus from Newton. The Society appointed a committee to inspect earlier letters. Its president was Newton, whose priority was at stake.

The committee's report appeared in 1713 as the *Commercium Epistolicum*. Newton had drafted the supposedly impartial judgment himself. It favoured Newton and treated Leibniz as a plagiarist. Today historians generally accept independent discovery: Newton developed his method earlier in private; Leibniz published his first. The most revealing fact is not who won the argument. It is how an institution can make its own president's case look like an independent verdict.
