# Two everyday words from one mathematician

Around the ninth century, al-Khwārizmī wrote a practical manual for solving equations in Baghdad. Its title included *al-jabr*, a way of restoring terms while balancing an equation. That became *algebra*.

His name took a different route. A Latin version of his work on Indian numerals circulated under a form of his name, *Algoritmi*. From it came *algorism* and eventually *algorithm*. So the two modern words reach us through two different books, one author's method and one author's name. His algebra used prose and geometric arguments, not our strings of symbols. What sounds like pure abstraction began as instructions for concrete problems, including inheritance and trade.

Q: Where do "algebra" and "algorithm" come from?
A: Both trace to al-Khwārizmī, through different works. *Algebra* comes from *al-jabr* in his equation manual’s title; *algorithm* developed from a Latin form of his name attached to his work on Indian numerals.
