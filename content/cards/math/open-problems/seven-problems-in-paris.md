# Seven problems in Paris: one prize refused, one claim pending

On 24 May 2000, at the Collège de France in Paris, the Clay Mathematics Institute put a million dollars on each of seven problems. The model was a century old: in August 1900, also in Paris, David Hilbert had presented the list that became his 23 problems.

Only one problem is on both lists: the Riemann hypothesis. It is still open.

The scoreboard since is short. Grigori Perelman solved the Poincaré conjecture and turned the prize down in 2010. Navier–Stokes is now "Active": after a blowup claimed in September 2026, Clay said it "has apparently been settled". The other five are still marked unsolved.

Collecting is harder than the fame suggests, because the official statements are narrow and precise.

## Rigor

In one line each, what the official descriptions ask:

1. **Riemann hypothesis.** Every nontrivial zero of $\zeta(s)$ has real part $\tfrac12$.
2. **P vs NP.** Is every problem whose solutions can be checked in polynomial time also solvable in polynomial time?
3. **Poincaré conjecture.** Every closed, simply connected 3-manifold is homeomorphic to $S^3$. Solved.
4. **Navier–Stokes.** For smooth, rapidly decaying data and no force on $\mathbb{R}^3$ (or on a periodic box), prove smooth solutions exist for all time; or exhibit a breakdown for some smooth data and smooth force.
5. **Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer.** For an elliptic curve $E$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, the rank of $E(\mathbb{Q})$ equals the order of vanishing of $L(E,s)$ at $s=1$.
6. **Hodge conjecture.** On a nonsingular complex projective variety, every Hodge class is a rational combination of classes of algebraic cycles.
7. **Yang–Mills and the mass gap.** For every compact simple gauge group, a quantum Yang–Mills theory on $\mathbb{R}^4$ exists and has a mass gap $\Delta>0$.

**The rules.** A solution must be published in a refereed venue and be generally accepted two years later before the Institute considers it. The Navier–Stokes statement accepts either answer, which is why a blowup construction counts.
