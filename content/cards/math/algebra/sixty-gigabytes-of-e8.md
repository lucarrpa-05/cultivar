# An answer about one symmetry group that fills 60 gigabytes

In March 2007 the Atlas of Lie Groups project finished computing the Kazhdan–Lusztig–Vogan polynomials for the split real form of $E_8$, the largest exceptional Lie group. The answer is a matrix with 453,060 rows and as many columns, whose entries are polynomials. It ran about 77 hours on a machine called Sage and occupies 60 gigabytes. $E_8$ itself has dimension 248.
