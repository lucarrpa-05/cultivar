# The theorem no single person has read

Every finite simple group is on one of four lists: the cyclic groups of prime order, the alternating groups from degree 5 up, sixteen infinite families of Lie type, and 26 sporadic groups that belong to no family at all. That sentence is the classification of finite simple groups, and it is the largest theorem ever proved.

The proof runs to tens of thousands of pages in several hundred journal articles by roughly a hundred authors, published mostly between 1955 and 2004. Nobody has read all of it. Nobody could.

Daniel Gorenstein announced it complete in 1983. He had been misinformed: the quasithin case was not finished. The gap stayed open for two decades, until Michael Aschbacher and Stephen Smith closed it in 2004 with 1,221 further pages.

There is now a second-generation project rewriting the whole argument as one coherent sequence of volumes, for the obvious reason. The community would like a proof that a human being could, in principle, check.
