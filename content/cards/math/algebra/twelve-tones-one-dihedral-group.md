# Remember group actions? A string quartet is one

Remember that an action is a group doing something to a set — the square's eight moves shuffling four corners. Take the set to be the twelve pitch classes, C through B, and take the group to be the two moves every composer already uses: transpose a line up by $n$ semitones, or invert it, flipping it upside down about some axis.

Twelve transpositions, twelve inversions. They compose, they undo, and inverting-transposing-inverting transposes the *other* way — the same relation that made the square's flips and turns refuse to commute. It is the dihedral group of order 24 acting on twelve notes. Two of Schoenberg's four row operations are exactly these; the other two just read the row backwards.

Then it gets strange. There is a second dihedral group of order 24 acting on a different set: the twenty-four major and minor triads, moved by the neo-Riemannian operations P, L and R that nineteenth-century harmony wanders through. Crans, Fiore and Satyendra showed in 2009 that the two actions are dual — each group is exactly the set of symmetries commuting with the other.

Here is the dictionary, in symbols.

## Rigor

Identify pitch classes with $\mathbb{Z}/12$ (C is 0). Define
$$T_n(x)=x+n,\qquad I_n(x)=-x+n \pmod{12}.$$
These 24 maps form a group under composition, with $T_1$ of order 12, $I_0$ of order 2, and $I_0T_1I_0=T_{-1}$ — the defining relation of the dihedral group of order 24. Its action on $\mathbb{Z}/12$ is the action of the symmetries of a regular 12-gon on its vertices: transitive, with each point stabilised by exactly one reflection.

Now let $S$ be the 24 consonant triads, written as ordered triples of pitch classes. The T/I group acts on $S$ simply transitively. So does the group generated by
$$P,\quad L,\quad R$$
(parallel, leading-tone exchange, relative), which is again dihedral of order 24. Whenever a group acts simply transitively, its centraliser in $\mathrm{Sym}(S)$ is again simply transitive and isomorphic to it — the left and right regular representations. Crans, Fiore and Satyendra check that these two groups are exactly each other's centralisers: the pitch-class picture and the chord picture are *dual* actions of the same group.

## Recall
type: mcq
Q: Why is the group generated by transposition and inversion dihedral rather than cyclic?
- [x] Inversion conjugates transposition into its inverse, $I_0T_1I_0=T_{-1}$ — exactly the relation between a flip and a turn of a polygon.
- [ ] Because there are 24 elements and 24 is even — order alone never distinguishes dihedral from cyclic; the cyclic group of order 24 exists too.
- [ ] Because inversion has order 12 — inversions are reflections and have order 2; the transpositions are the ones of order 12.
