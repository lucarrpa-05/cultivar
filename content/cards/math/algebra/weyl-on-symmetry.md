# The sentence Weyl chose to open with

Hermann Weyl gave four lectures at Princeton in 1951, just before retiring, and published them in 1952 as *Symmetry*. It opens:

> "Symmetry, as wide or as narrow as you may define its meaning, is one idea by which man through the ages has tried to comprehend and create order, beauty, and perfection."

He then spends the book narrowing it on purpose, until "symmetry" means a group of transformations. That opening line is a warning label: the mathematical meaning is a choice, kept because you can compute with it.
