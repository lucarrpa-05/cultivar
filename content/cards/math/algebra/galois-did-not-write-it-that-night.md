# Galois did not write it all the night before the duel

The duel is real. 30 May 1832, a gunshot to the abdomen, dead the next morning at the Hôpital Cochin, aged twenty. The letter he wrote to his friend Auguste Chevalier on 29 May is real too.

What is invented is the boy conjuring group theory out of nothing in one night. Galois had been working on the solvability of equations since 1829, when he was seventeen. Cauchy handled his first submission to the Académie and nothing came of it. A memoir sent for the Grand Prix in 1830 went to Fourier, who died months later; the manuscript was never found. Poisson returned a third in July 1831 as not clear enough to judge. Galois spent most of the following year in prison for political offences.

So the letter is not a miracle. It is a summary, written by someone who expected to die, of work that had already been rejected three times by people who could have read it.

Tony Rothman took the legend apart in the *American Mathematical Monthly* in 1982. The true story is worse, and better.
