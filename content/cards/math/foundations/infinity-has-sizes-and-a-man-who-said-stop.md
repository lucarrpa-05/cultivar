# Infinity has sizes, and the man who said stop

Leopold Kronecker believed mathematics was about whole numbers and things you could actually construct in finitely many steps. Then his former student Georg Cantor published, in 1874, a proof that the real numbers cannot be listed — that there are strictly more of them than there are counting numbers.

To Kronecker this was not a theorem, it was theology. He held up Cantor's papers at *Crelle's Journal*, opposed every application Cantor made to Berlin, and is reported to have called him a scientific charlatan, a renegade and a corrupter of youth. Cantor, who had been reaching for a professorship he never got, suffered his first documented breakdown in 1884 and recurring bouts until his death in a sanatorium in 1918.

The tempting story — Kronecker drove Cantor mad — is too neat; historians now read the episodes as bipolar illness that the hostility did not help. What is certain is the verdict. Hilbert, lecturing at Münster in 1925 and in print the next year: *Aus dem Paradies, das Cantor uns geschaffen, soll uns niemand vertreiben können.* No one shall drive us out of the paradise Cantor created for us.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What did Cantor prove in 1874 that Kronecker could not accept?
A: That the real numbers are uncountable — no list indexed by $1,2,3,\dots$ can contain them all — so "infinite" splits into genuinely different sizes. For Kronecker, objects you cannot construct in finitely many steps were not mathematical objects at all.
