# The man who named topology lost the strip

Johann Benedict Listing studied under Gauss and in 1847 published *Vorstudien zur Topologie* — the first appearance in print of the word "topology", though he had been using it in letters for about a decade. Before him the subject went by Leibniz's phrase, *analysis situs*.

In 1858 he built a strip of paper with a half twist, joined the ends, and found a surface with one side and one edge. The same year, independently, so did August Ferdinand Möbius. Listing went further than Möbius did, working out what happens with two twists, three, four — he called them paradromic rings. Who got there first is still argued about.

The surface is universally called the Möbius strip. The field is universally called topology. One man supplied the word for everything and the name of nothing.

A coda that flattens both claims: Roman mosaics from the third century show ribbons with an odd number of coils. Whether the artists meant it is anyone's guess.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What did Listing contribute, and what carries his name?
A: He coined "topology" (in print, 1847), found the one-sided strip in 1858 independently of Möbius, and generalised it to strips with more twists. The word is his; the strip is named after Möbius.
