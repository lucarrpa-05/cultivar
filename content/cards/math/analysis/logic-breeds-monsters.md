# Logic sometimes breeds monsters

"Logic sometimes breeds monsters. For half a century there has been springing up a host of weird functions, which seem to strive to have as little resemblance as possible to honest functions that are of some use."

Poincaré, *Science et Méthode* (1908), book II chapter 2, in Maitland's 1914 translation. He was no reactionary; he had turned up plenty of wild objects himself. His complaint was pedagogical: start from the general case and you start the beginner wrestling with monstrosities.

A century on, the monsters are still taught last.
