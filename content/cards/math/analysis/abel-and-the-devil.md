# Abel, 1826, and the devil in the translation

Abel's most quoted sentence calls divergent series "an invention of the devil". His collected works print the January 1826 letter to Holmboe more mildly: "Divergent series are in general something fatal, and it is a disgrace to base any proof on them." Arild Stubhaug's biography keeps the devil — "the work of the Devil". One letter, two translators, one flourish.

He had reason: divergent series were being manipulated freely and producing contradictions. The epilogue is the joke — Borel, Cesàro and Hardy built summability theory, and the fatal things became instruments.
