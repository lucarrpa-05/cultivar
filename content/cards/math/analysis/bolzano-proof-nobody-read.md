# The proof nobody read for fifty years

In 1817 a priest in Prague published a pamphlet with a title longer than some of its proofs: *Rein analytischer Beweis des Lehrsatzes, dass zwischen je zwey Werthen, die ein entgegengesetztes Resultat gewähren, wenigstens eine reelle Wurzel der Gleichung liege.* Purely analytic proof that between two values of opposite sign there lies a root.

The word doing the work is *analytic*. Everyone already believed the intermediate value theorem; they believed it because a curve that starts below the axis and ends above it obviously crosses. Bernard Bolzano refused the picture and built the proof out of inequalities and suprema alone — the first time anyone treated continuity as something to argue from rather than to look at.

Then almost nothing. He was stripped of his professorship on 24 December 1819 for refusing to recant his politics, published outside the mainstream, and was read by nearly no one. Weierstrass reached the same ideas about fifty years later; the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem carried only Weierstrass's name until Bolzano's papers resurfaced.

Being right early is not the same as being first.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What was actually new in Bolzano's 1817 pamphlet, given that everyone already believed the theorem?
A: The method. He proved the intermediate value theorem from the definition of continuity and the completeness of the reals — with no appeal to a drawn curve. It is the first analytic proof of a "geometrically obvious" statement.
