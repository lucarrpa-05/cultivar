# What did it cost to read one human genome in 2001?

The Human Genome Project took thirteen years and roughly \$2.7 billion, but that budget covered technology development, model organisms and ethics work as well as sequencing. The cleaner question is the one the US National Human Genome Research Institute has tracked ever since: the production cost of sequencing *one more* human genome, start to finish, at a funded centre.

Three guesses. What was it in September 2001? What was it two decades later? And what happened between October 2007 and January 2008 that made this the one technology curve people compare favourably to Moore's law?

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: The three numbers.
A: September 2001: \$95,263,072. May 2022: \$524.63 — a factor of roughly 180,000 in twenty-one years. The jump: October 2007 was the last Sanger data point at \$7.1 million; by January 2008, with centres switched to next-generation sequencers, it was \$3.1 million, and NHGRI describes what followed as a "sudden and profound out-pacing of Moore's Law". Caveat: these are production costs at funded centres, not a price you could pay, and they exclude interpreting what the sequence means.
