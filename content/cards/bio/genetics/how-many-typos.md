# How many typos did you start life with?

Three billion letters had to be copied to make each of the cells you began as, and copying is never perfect. So: how many single-letter changes do you carry that neither of your parents had? Pick an order of magnitude — ten, a hundred, a thousand, a million.

Then the harder question, the one with an actual mechanism behind it. Did more of those new mutations come from your mother or from your father? And what would you have to know about your parents to sharpen the estimate?

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: How many new mutations, and from which parent?
A: Around 60–70. Sequencing 78 Icelandic parent–child trios, Kong et al. (2012) measured about 1.2 × 10⁻⁸ new mutations per base per generation — roughly 63 per child when the father was 29.7. Most come from the father: sperm are produced by continuing cell division throughout life while a woman's eggs are not, so the count rises by about two mutations for every extra year of the father's age.
