# How much grass is a lion?

Energy climbs a food chain badly. Almost everything an antelope eats is spent staying warm, moving, and being an antelope; only a small fraction ends up as antelope that a lion could eat.

So: to add one kilogram of new lion to the savannah, roughly how many kilograms of plants have to be grown? Work it out with a single efficiency figure applied twice.

Then the question the arithmetic answers for free: why is there no land animal that makes its living by eating lions?

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: The number, and why food chains are short.
A: The rule of thumb is that about 10% of the energy at one level becomes biomass at the next — Lindeman's 1942 figure, though he never called it a law and reported efficiencies anywhere from 0.1% to 37.5%. Grass → antelope → lion is two steps, so roughly 100 kg of plants per kilogram of lion; a lion-eater would need about 1,000. Each step divides the available energy by ten, so by the fourth or fifth level there is not enough left to feed a viable population. That is also why top predators are rare and need enormous territories.
