# The envelope that ended twenty years of not publishing

Darwin had the argument by 1838 and a 230-page essay by 1844, with written instructions telling his wife how to publish it if he died. Then he left it in a drawer for fourteen years and wrote about barnacles instead.

On 18 June 1858 a package arrived from the Malay Archipelago. Alfred Russel Wallace, a collector fourteen years younger who funded himself by selling specimens, had worked out the same mechanism during a fever on Ternate that February and posted it to the one famous naturalist he thought might pass it along. Darwin wrote to Lyell that day: he had been forestalled.

The compromise was a joint reading at the Linnean Society on 1 July 1858. Neither man was in the room. Wallace was still in the archipelago; Darwin was burying his infant son, dead of scarlet fever three days before. Nobody reacted. The society's president, Thomas Bell, wrote in his address the next year that 1858 "has not, indeed, been marked by any of those striking discoveries which at once revolutionize" a field.

*On the Origin of Species* went on sale sixteen months later.
