# The map of your body in your head has enormous lips

In the 1930s Wilder Penfield operated on people with severe epilepsy while they were awake. The scalp takes local anaesthetic and the brain itself feels nothing, so he could touch a stimulating electrode to the exposed cortex and ask what the patient noticed. Here, and the thumb tingles. Two millimetres over, and it is the index finger. He was trying to find tissue he could safely remove; what he got was a map.

Running down a strip of cortex, the body is laid out in order — but with the proportions wrong. Lips, tongue and hands occupy huge territories; the trunk and the thigh get slivers. Cortex is handed out according to how finely a part must be sensed and controlled, not by how big it is. Your fingertips are expensive; your back is cheap.

The clean little figure in textbooks oversells it. Penfield himself noted heavy overlap between regions, and precision scanning in 2023 found the strip is interrupted by patches that belong to a whole-body action network rather than to any single limb.
