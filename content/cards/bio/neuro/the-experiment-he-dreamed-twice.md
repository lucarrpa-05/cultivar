# The experiment he had to dream twice

In 1921 the argument about how one nerve talks to the next was stuck. One camp said electricity jumped the gap. The other said a chemical crossed it, and had no way to show it.

On the night before Easter Sunday, Otto Loewi dreamed the experiment that would settle it. He woke, scribbled it on a scrap of paper by the bed, and went back to sleep. In the morning the note was illegible and he could not reconstruct it. The following night the same dream came back. This time he got dressed and went to the laboratory at three in the morning.

Two frog hearts in saline. Stimulate the vagus nerve on the first and it slows. Take the fluid bathing that heart and pour it over the second heart, which has no nerve attached. The second heart slows too. Something had leaked out of the first one — he called it *Vagusstoff*, "vagus stuff". It turned out to be acetylcholine.

Nobel Prize, 1936.

## Recall
type: mcq
Q: What did the fluid transfer between two frog hearts actually prove?
- [x] That the nerve's effect is carried by a released substance — a heart with no nerve attached responded to the bathing fluid alone.
- [ ] That electrical signals can travel through saline — an electrical explanation is exactly what the fluid transfer rules out, since no current path existed.
- [ ] That the vagus nerve slows the heart — that was already known; the question was by what means.
- [ ] That acetylcholine is the universal transmitter — Loewi identified an effect, not the molecule, and there turned out to be dozens of transmitters.
