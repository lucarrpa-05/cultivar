# You are not outnumbered ten to one

The claim that bacteria outnumber your own cells ten to one came from a rough estimate published in 1972, and got repeated for four decades because it is a lovely thing to say. In 2016 Sender, Fuchs and Milo did the arithmetic properly for a 70 kg reference man: about 3.8 × 10¹³ bacteria against about 3.0 × 10¹³ human cells. The real ratio is close to one to one — and a single visit to the bathroom shifts it.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: What is the actual ratio of bacterial to human cells in the body, and why did the old number survive so long?
A: Roughly 1.3 to 1, not 10 to 1 — about 3.8 × 10¹³ bacteria to 3.0 × 10¹³ human cells (Sender, Fuchs and Milo, 2016). The old figure was a rough 1972 estimate that nobody rechecked, because it was memorable, repeatable and flattering to the field.
