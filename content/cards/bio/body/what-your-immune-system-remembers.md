# What your immune system remembers, and what it cannot

Chickenpox usually happens once; flu can come back. Your immune system remembers particular features of a pathogen, not a promise that every future version will look the same.

During infection or vaccination, lymphocytes that recognize those features multiply. Some persist as memory cells; long-lived plasma cells can keep making antibodies. On a later encounter, that stored response can start faster.

Influenza complicates the bargain. Its surface proteins change through *antigenic drift*, so yesterday's antibodies may fit less well. Protection after vaccination can also wane with time. That is why flu vaccines are reviewed and offered each season: both the target and your protection can change.
