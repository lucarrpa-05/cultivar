# "Hardly anyone takes anyone else's data analyses seriously"

Edward Leamer, halfway through his 1983 *American Economic Review* broadside: "Hardly anyone takes data analyses seriously. Or perhaps more accurately, hardly anyone takes anyone else's data analyses seriously. Like elaborately plumed birds who have long since lost the ability to procreate but not the desire, we preen and strut and display our t-values."

His target: specification search — fitting the regression many ways, reporting the one that behaved. Twenty-seven years later Angrist and Pischke borrowed his title to announce the fix: argue about where the variation came from.
