# Rationality has two blades, and one of them is the world

Nobody has ever met the decision-maker in the textbook: the one who knows every option, ranks them all, takes the best. Herbert Simon spent fifty years insisting this is not a small idealisation you can wave through. It is the wrong starting point.

His replacement was *satisficing*. Search until an option clears a threshold you already call good enough, then stop looking. A firm hunts an acceptable return, not the maximum. You did not view every apartment in Bogotá before signing a lease, and you were right not to.

Simon's best image came in 1990: rational behaviour is shaped by a scissors whose two blades are the structure of the environment and the computational limits of the mind. Study one blade and you cannot explain the cut. Which is why "people are irrational" is usually the lazy diagnosis — half the time it was the environment that moved, not the mind.
