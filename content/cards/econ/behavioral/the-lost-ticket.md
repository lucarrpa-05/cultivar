# Lose the ticket and you go home. Lose the cash and you watch.

You paid \$10 for a theatre ticket. At the door you find the ticket is gone. Would you pay \$10 again? In Tversky and Kahneman's 1981 study, 46% said yes.

Different group, different wording. You are going to the theatre, admission is \$10, paid at the door. On the way in you discover you have lost a \$10 note. Would you still buy the ticket? Here 88% said yes.

You are \$10 poorer in both worlds and the play costs the same in both. The only thing that changed is where the loss gets filed. A lost ticket goes into the account marked *this evening at the theatre*, which makes the evening cost \$20 — an absurd price for a play. A lost banknote goes into general funds, where it makes you very slightly poorer and nothing else happens.

Money is fungible. The accounts in your head are not, and they are the ones doing the deciding.
