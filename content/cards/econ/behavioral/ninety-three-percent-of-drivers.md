# 93% of drivers are in the better half

Ola Svenson asked American drivers in 1981 to rank themselves against the rest of the sample. 93% placed themselves in the top half for driving skill. The same trick works for teaching quality, popularity, and — best of all — for the ability to judge oneself without bias.

What it is not is proof that people are stupid. Ask about a genuinely difficult skill, juggling or programming, and the better-than-average effect can flip into a *worse*-than-average effect. People are not uniformly vain. They are bad at knowing where the middle is.
