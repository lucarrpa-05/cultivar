# Write down five ranges you are 90% sure about

A test you can score on yourself. Answer each of these with a *range*, not a number, and make the range wide enough that you are 90% confident the true value falls inside it.

The length of the Nile, in kilometres. The year Mozart was born. The number of African member states of the United Nations. The gestation of an Asian elephant, in days. The air distance from Bogotá to Madrid, in kilometres.

Five questions at 90% each: a calibrated person misses half of one. Write your ranges down before looking anything up, count your misses, and then ask what that miss rate does to a budget, a forecast, or a thesis timeline.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: How many of your five 90% ranges contained the truth?
A: Nile ≈ 6,650 km; Mozart 1756; 54 African countries; Asian elephant ≈ 640 days; Bogotá–Madrid ≈ 8,010 km. Almost nobody misses only half of one. In the classic calibration studies, people's 90% intervals contain the truth closer to 50% of the time, and warning or training them barely moves it. The repair is not knowing more. It is widening the interval until it feels embarrassing — which is roughly where honest 90% coverage begins.
