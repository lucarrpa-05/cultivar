# Investors sell what is rising and keep what is falling

Terrance Odean obtained the trading records of 10,000 accounts at a discount brokerage covering 1987 to 1993 and asked one question: when somebody sells, which of their holdings do they pick?

They pick the winners. Outside December, investors realised about 14.8% of the positions sitting on a gain and about 9.8% of those sitting on a loss. A stock that is up is roughly half again as likely to be sold as one that is down.

The name is the disposition effect, and it is backwards even before you argue about returns: in the United States realised losses are deductible and realised gains are taxed, so the tax-efficient order is precisely the reverse. December is the exception: the order flips to 10.8% of gains and 12.8% of losses, when the tax deadline finally outweighs the reluctance.

Prospect theory supplies the rest. Selling a loser converts a paper loss into a real one and prices it on the steep arm of the value function. Holding on keeps it hypothetical.
