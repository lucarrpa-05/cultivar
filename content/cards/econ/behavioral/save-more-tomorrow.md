# The savings plan that only asks for money you do not have yet

Saving more hurts now and pays later, which is the one shape human beings handle worst. Richard Thaler and Shlomo Benartzi's answer was to stop fighting that and design around it.

Their SMarT plan — Save More Tomorrow — asks employees to commit today to raising their retirement contribution *at their next pay rise*, and at every rise after that. Three biases switch sides. Present bias: the cost sits in the future, where it barely registers. Loss aversion: contributions climb only when take-home pay climbs, so nobody ever watches a paycheque shrink. Inertia: the escalation continues by default, and stopping it requires an action nobody gets around to.

At the first company that ran it, employees who joined went from saving 3.5% of pay to 13.6% by their fourth raise. Published in the *Journal of Political Economy* in 2004, it became one of the most copied ideas in retirement policy — and the cleanest case of a nudge that recruits a bias instead of lecturing it.

## Recall
type: reveal
Q: Which three behavioural forces does Save More Tomorrow recruit rather than fight?
A: Present bias — the cost is deferred. Loss aversion — the contribution rises only out of a raise, so take-home pay never falls. Inertia — the escalation runs by default until you opt out.
