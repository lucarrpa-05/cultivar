# Cab drivers quit early on exactly the days they should stay out

Standard labour supply says work longer when the wage is high. New York taxi drivers face an hourly take that swings with rain, conventions and broken subway lines, varies enormously between days and barely at all within one — which makes them close to a natural experiment on the question.

Camerer, Babcock, Loewenstein and Thaler went through three samples of trip sheets in 1997 and found the elasticity pointing the wrong way, most sharply among inexperienced drivers: on good days they went home early. The story is a daily income target. Hit roughly the usual take, feel finished, park the cab — and on a slow day keep driving into the night to get there. The target is a reference point, and falling short of it is coded as a loss.

Treat this as a live argument rather than a settled fact. Henry Farber, working with millions of electronic trip records, gets elasticities that are usually positive. Thakral and Tô found in 2021 that income targeting survives once the reference point is allowed to drift during the day. Three teams, one question, twenty-five years.
