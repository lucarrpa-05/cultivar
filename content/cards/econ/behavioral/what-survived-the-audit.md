# The scoreboard, and why it is not an argument for cynicism

Three audits, three numbers. The Open Science Collaboration reran 97 psychology findings in 2015 and got 36% to replicate, with surviving effects about half their original size. Camerer and colleagues reran 18 laboratory economics experiments in 2016: 61%. They then took the social-science papers published in *Nature* and *Science* between 2010 and 2015: around 62%, again at roughly half strength.

Now the specifics, because averages hide the interesting part. Ego depletion — the idea that self-control runs on a depletable fuel — was tested by 23 labs in 2016 and by 36 labs in 2021, and both times the pooled effect was indistinguishable from zero. Several social priming results went the same way. Power posing lost its author's own support.

Loss aversion, anchoring and present bias came through. So did most of the ultimatum-game and public-goods findings. The lesson is not that the field was a fraud. It is that effect sizes published before preregistration were systematically inflated, and the ones built on many independent replications were not.
