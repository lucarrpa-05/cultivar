# Austria says yes 99.98% of the time. Germany says yes 12%.

Two neighbours, similar hospitals, similar culture, and effective consent to organ donation that differs by nearly ninety points. Austria sits at 99.98%. Germany sits at about 12%.

The difference is one line on a form. Austria presumes you consent unless you opt out; Germany asks you to opt in. Johnson and Goldstein laid the pattern out in *Science* in 2003 across a set of European countries and the split is brutally clean: opt-in countries cluster low, opt-out countries press against the ceiling. Nobody is choosing differently. The box starts in a different position.

That is what a default is — the outcome of doing nothing, in a world where doing nothing is the most popular action there is.

The honest caveat, which this literature took years to say loudly: consent on paper is not a transplant. Greece has presumed consent and one of Europe's lowest donation rates. The form is necessary. It is nowhere near sufficient.
