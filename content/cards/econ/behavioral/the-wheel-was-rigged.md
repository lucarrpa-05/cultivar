# The wheel of fortune was rigged, and it moved every answer

Tversky and Kahneman spun a wheel of fortune in front of their subjects. The wheel was rigged: it stopped on 10, or it stopped on 65. Then they asked what percentage of the countries in the United Nations are African.

The group that had watched it land on 10 answered 25, on median. The group that had watched it land on 65 answered 45. A number everyone had just seen a wheel produce — a number with exactly zero information in it — shifted estimates by twenty points.

That is anchoring, published in *Science* in 1974, and it is one of the sturdiest things in the field. When the big multi-lab replication projects went back through the classics after 2011, anchoring survived; ego depletion and several priming results did not. The uncomfortable part is not that people are influenced. It is that warning them about the anchor, or paying them for accuracy, barely helps.
